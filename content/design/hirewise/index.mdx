## 01 · Job searching had become unsafe as well as slow

Job searching is spread across job boards, documents, spreadsheets, messaging platforms and interview-preparation tools. Candidates spend more time filtering than applying, rewrite similar applications without knowing if the changes help, and lose track of what they sent.

The emotional cost is equally important. Rejection without feedback, recruiter ghosting, misleading job descriptions and fake postings make the process feel unsafe and demoralising.

## 02 · What the research changed

I analysed discussions from job-seeking and recruitment communities on Reddit, LinkedIn, Glassdoor and other social platforms, and compared LinkedIn, Indeed, Teal, Huntr, Jobscan and Big Interview. Five patterns kept returning: information overload, the tailoring paradox, the tracking gap, a trust crisis, and emotional burnout.

{/* NEEDS INPUT: roughly how many posts or threads you reviewed. */}

The original direction emphasised tailoring and tracking. The trust crisis changed it: protecting candidates from exploitative or misleading listings was equally valuable and more differentiating.

<CalloutBox title="Core research insight">
  Productivity alone would not solve the problem. Candidates needed a product that combined efficiency with protection, transparency, and visible progress.
</CalloutBox>

## 03 · Decision 1: Warn on the job card, and leave the final call to the candidate

Fake postings and engagement-farming recruiters are a safety problem, not merely an inconvenience, so HireWise checks a job description before candidates invest time or disclose personal information. The Red Flag Detector looks for:

- Compensation that conflicts with the required experience level
- Vague responsibilities or missing company information
- Unverified employers
- Requests for sensitive information too early
- Unpaid test projects with disproportionate scope
- Entry-level roles carrying unrealistic experience requirements

Warnings appear on the job card in three states: verified, caution and high-risk. An expandable explanation shows why a role was flagged, and the candidate can continue after acknowledging the warning. I chose to warn rather than decide for them, because candidates valued explanations but still wanted final control.

## 04 · Decision 2: Turn interview practice into a feedback loop, not a score

Generic interview lists rarely reflect a candidate's actual role, seniority or industry. Questions are grouped into behavioural, technical, case-study and company-specific categories, and respond to the job title, seniority, industry and the saved job description. The mock interview removes unrelated navigation and focuses on the current question, with a timer for realistic pressure and voice-to-text, pause and resume controls.

A score alone does not help candidates improve. After each session, they see question-by-question feedback, their answer next to specific suggestions and a model response, the skills they demonstrated and the ones that need stronger evidence, and clear next steps. Strengths and improvement areas are visually separated, and the language stays growth-focused.

## 05 · Tracking that updates itself

Manual trackers often fail because updating them becomes another task. HireWise brings discovery and tracking into one flow so applications can be recorded automatically. Timeline, kanban and list views answer three questions: where is each application now, what needs attention next, and am I making progress?

## 08 · What I learned

### Emotional design is functional design

Job search products operate in a context of rejection and uncertainty. Progress states, feedback language, reminders and errors must acknowledge that emotional reality.

### Fragmentation is the real competitor

Candidates did not need another isolated tool. The stronger proposition was reducing the number of disconnected systems required to discover, apply, track and prepare.
