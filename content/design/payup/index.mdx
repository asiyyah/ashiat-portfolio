## 01 · Getting paid was held together with separate tools

Getting paid is one of the most important parts of freelance work, yet the process is often held together with separate invoice tools, spreadsheets, email threads and manual reminders. As client volume grows, freelancers can lose track of what has been sent, what is overdue, and which follow-up should happen next.

I ran an online survey of freelance professionals across locations and disciplines, covering younger freelancers establishing their businesses, professionals balancing freelance work with other commitments, and experienced independents growing toward agency models.

## 02 · The opening: between marketplaces and accounting software

I reviewed Upwork, Freelancer, Harvest, Wave and Stripe. Marketplace tools connect payments to work sourced inside their ecosystems, while accounting and payment products often bring more complexity than an independent professional needs. PayUp sits between them: focused enough for everyday freelance work, but complete enough to replace manual tracking.

That set the scope to four connected capabilities: invoice creation and sending, automated payment reminders, flexible payment collection, and payment tracking and reporting. Full accounting and project management stayed out, to protect the core experience from unnecessary complexity.

## 03 · Decision 1: Decide the follow-up while you send the invoice

Following up on late payments is uncomfortable and easy to postpone. Instead of making reminders a separate tool, I put Set Reminder next to Send Invoice, so the follow-up is decided while the freelancer is still thinking about that client. They can return later to configure the date, frequency, delivery channel and message.

Default messages provide a quick starting point, while custom copy preserves the freelancer's relationship with each client. Reminder status and client responses stay connected to the invoice instead of becoming another email thread to manage.

## 04 · Decision 2: Every payment updates the invoice, even ones that happen offline

Freelancers can connect familiar gateways such as Stripe, PayPal, Square and Paystack, then offer clients bank, card and gateway options without leaving the invoicing workflow. That matters for freelancers working from Nigeria with international clients. After a transaction, the related invoice and payment states update, so there is less to reconcile by hand.

Not every payment goes through a gateway, so I added a focused Record Payment modal that asks only for the invoice, date, payment method and resulting status. Offline and external payments end up in the same record as everything else.

## 05 · Decision 3: Status a first-time freelancer can read

PayUp needed to work for freelancers sending their first professional invoice and for experienced independents managing a growing client list. I used familiar table patterns, plain-language labels, consistent paid, pending and overdue statuses, labelled totals, confirmation points and progressively disclosed settings, so the most important information is visible at a glance and creation and follow-up flows stay short.

## 07 · What I learned

Designing for money required more than adding financial features: every status, action and consequence had to be immediately understandable. Supporting different payment providers also showed me that accessibility includes cultural context, regional payment habits and different levels of financial confidence.
